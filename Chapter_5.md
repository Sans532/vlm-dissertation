# Chapter 5: Conclusions

## 5.1 Answering the Research Question

This dissertation asked a single two-part question: can vision-language models judge human skill level from video, zero-shot, and if they fail, is the failure perceptual or linguistic? The answer to the first part is no. Across four models, two video-input paradigms, four prompt formats, two viewpoints, three frame budgets, two trimming conditions and five activity domains, no zero-shot configuration produces a reliable, non-degenerate above-chance result on four-class skill assessment, and the one genuine above-chance result obtained anywhere in the project — Gemini's 76% on binary climbing, egocentric view — is an isolated exception rather than a demonstrated capability. The answer to the second part is that the failure is linguistic, and specifically located at the language-generation step rather than anywhere upstream of it. A linear probe over frozen CLIP features classifies the same clips at 67% four-class accuracy where every VLM tested scores 24–25%, which means the discriminative visual signal is present and linearly accessible in the representation before it ever reaches a language model. A text-only control given genuine expert commentary describing the performance, with no video at all, collapses to the same chance-level band. Vision alone succeeds; vision-to-language fails; language-to-language fails. The bottleneck is not what the models see, or even what they are told — it is what the language-generation head does with either.

## 5.2 Achievement of the Objectives

The dissertation was organised as eleven sequential ablations plus a final localisation control; the eight that matter for assessing whether the objectives were met are treated here as H1–H8. H3 (viewpoint) and H5 (prompt format) were confirmed: camera perspective and output format materially change collapse behaviour and class spread. H7 and H8 — the CLIP-probe localisation and the text-only control — were confirmed as designed and jointly deliver the dissertation's central claim. H1 (baseline feasibility), H2 (frame budget), H4 (trimming) and H6 (cross-domain generalisation) were rejected: models do not reliably classify skill zero-shot, more frames do not help, removing idle footage does not repair the Novice default, and the collapse holds across climbing, dance, basketball, surgical suturing and a mixed activity set. Both outcomes count as completion of the objectives. A hypothesis-testing dissertation is not required to have every hypothesis confirmed; the null result on H4 is not a shortfall against the plan, it is evidence — it rules out idle-footage confusion as the source of the Novice prior and, combined with H6's negative transfer result, narrows the explanation to something more fundamental than a fixable data artefact, which is precisely what motivated the H7/H8 localisation controls that produced the dissertation's strongest finding.

## 5.3 Contributions

Three findings from this work would stand on their own if read outside the rest of the dissertation.

**The localisation of the failure to language generation.** The H7/H8 triangulation — a frozen-CLIP linear probe succeeding where every VLM fails, paired with a text-only control that fails even when handed the correct answer in prose — is a stronger and more specific claim than "VLMs are bad at this task." It rules out the visual encoder, rules out the raw availability of the evidence in language form, and leaves only the model's ability to convert either into the correct categorical judgment. This distinguishes the finding from a benchmark score and gives it a mechanism.

**The Video-LLaVA context-ceiling arithmetic.** Sixteen frames at 256 visual tokens each is exactly 4,096 tokens — the full context window of a Vicuna-7B backbone — before a single word of prompt text is added. This is not a property of this dissertation's specific results; it is a general architectural constraint that applies to any fixed-token-per-frame video-language model with a comparable context budget, and it means "increase the frame count" is not a free lever for such architectures regardless of the task being evaluated.

**Paired accuracy-and-distribution reporting as a methodological convention.** The JIGSAWS result in this dissertation — 65.5% accuracy from a model that answered "Novice" on every single clip against an imbalanced 19/10 ground truth — is the concrete demonstration of why a bare accuracy figure is not trustworthy evidence of discriminative judgment on a closed-label task. Reporting per-class recall and the predicted-label distribution alongside every accuracy number is a transferable convention for anyone evaluating a generative model against a small fixed label set, independent of skill assessment or of video at all.

## 5.4 Limitations

Beyond the specific caveats already stated in Chapter 4, three limitations bound the scope of these conclusions at the level of the whole project. First, the evaluation is zero-shot throughout; no model was fine-tuned or given few-shot exemplars, so nothing here speaks to whether supervised alignment of the visual representation to the label vocabulary would close the gap the CLIP probe identifies — the claim is that the zero-shot language-generation step fails, not that no language-generation step could succeed. Second, architectural diversity across the four evaluated models is more limited than four independent systems suggests: two of them, Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B, share a Qwen lineage, so the finding is properly triangulated across three architecturally distinct families — Qwen, Vicuna-based Video-LLaVA, and Gemini — rather than four. Third, the ground-truth labels themselves are participant-level judgments: Ego-Exo4D's proficiency-demonstrator annotations assign a skill level to a person based on training history and demonstrated competence, and that single label is then attached to every take that participant recorded. A take-level departure from a participant's typical performance is invisible to this scheme, which means the benchmark's ground truth carries some irreducible uncertainty about the specific clip being judged, not only about the model judging it.

## 5.5 Future Work

Two directions follow directly from findings already established here, rather than from a general call for more models or larger benchmarks. First, given that a linear probe over frozen CLIP features already recovers 67% accuracy from the same visual representations the VLMs fail to use, the natural next experiment is supervised alignment — fine-tuning only the projection layer or the language head against proficiency labels — to test whether the gap identified by the probe is closable without touching the vision encoder at all, and how much supervision that requires. Second, the language-generation bottleneck was established for one evaluative-judgment task: physical skill. Whether it holds for other tasks that share skill assessment's structure — a visual scene, a continuous underlying quality, and a categorical verdict the model must commit to, such as aesthetic quality judgment or visual safety-compliance checking — or whether it is specific to the fine-grained kinematic evidence that distinguishes a novice from an expert climber, is an open question this dissertation's method is directly suited to answering: the same three-control triangulation (diagnostic, linear probe, text-only) applies unchanged to any task in that family.
